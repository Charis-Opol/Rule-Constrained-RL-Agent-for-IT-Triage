{\bfseries\fontsize{11}{13}\selectfont Title: Rule-Constrained Reinforcement Learning for Ambiguity-Aware IT Incident Triage}

\head{Problem and research objective}
\lead{Problem.} When an IT incident is reported, staff must act before they know the cause, and the first evidence is often incomplete or contradictory. They can investigate further, try a likely fix, or hand the incident to a more senior person. AI tools for IT operations (AIOps) give one answer per ticket: they cannot ask for more evidence, signal that they are unsure, or check their suggestion against the organisation's rules~[1].

\lead{Objective.} To build and evaluate an AI agent that learns when to investigate further, when to recommend a fix the rules allow, and when to escalate the incident to a human expert.

\head{What the research shows, the gap and my contribution}
\lead{Systematic review} (PRISMA 2020; Scopus, IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library; 4,399 records screened down to \textbf{34 included studies})~[4]:
\begin{itemize}
  \item 17 of the 34 studies let the agent gather information, but only 7 include escalation to a person, as in learning to defer~[3]. \textbf{Only one combines all three choices}, and there escalation is fixed by design, not learned.
  \item Rule enforcement is mostly a reward penalty, which does not stop rule breaks~[2]. Only 5 studies use a hard rule filter (``shield''), and only 3 say what happens to a blocked decision.
  \item Decision quality is measured in 31 studies; escalation in only 5 and rule compliance in 4.
\end{itemize}
\lead{Gap.} No reported agent \emph{learns} to investigate, act or escalate for IT service incidents under explicit rules, or is evaluated on all four measures as the evidence gets worse.

\lead{Contribution.} (1)~An IT incident simulator built from UCU practice, with adjustable evidence quality. (2)~An agent with all three choices and a separate rule checker. (3)~A comparison with six strategies on four measures.
