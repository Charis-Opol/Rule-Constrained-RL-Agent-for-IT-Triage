Information technology (IT) support teams must decide how to handle an incident before its cause is known: investigate further, apply a fix, or pass the incident to a person. This review examines how reinforcement learning and related sequential methods are applied to IT incident handling, which of these three decisions their agents make, how operational rules are enforced, and how the agents are evaluated. Following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guideline, searches of Scopus, IEEE Xplore and the ACM Digital Library on 23 October 2026 returned 4,399 records. After 73 duplicates were removed and 4,195 records were filtered out automatically, 131 were screened, 40 reports were assessed in full, and 34 studies (2020--2026) were included and appraised with an eight-item checklist. Four studies represent the hidden incident state with an explicit belief. Seventeen give the agent an information-gathering action and 7 include escalation to a person; only one combines all three decisions, and its escalation is fixed by design rather than learned. Five use a hard filter or shield, and 3 report what happens to a blocked or uncertain decision. Decision quality is measured in 31 studies, escalation behaviour in 5, constraint compliance in 4, and robustness in 15. The evidence is recent (27 studies from 2025 or 2026) and of uneven quality: 12 meet at most two checklist items. No study reports a single policy that learns to investigate, act or escalate for IT service incidents under explicit rules.
