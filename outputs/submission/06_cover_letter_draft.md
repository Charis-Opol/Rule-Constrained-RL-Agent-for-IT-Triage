**Submission: "Reinforcement Learning for Investigating, Acting and Escalating in IT Incident Triage and Response: A Systematic Literature Review"**

Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript for publication as a review article in *Artificial Intelligence Review*.

IT support and security teams often have to decide what to do with an incident before its cause is known: investigate further, apply a fix, or escalate it to a person. Reinforcement learning can treat this as a sequence of decisions, but the relevant work is spread across IT operations, security operations and safe reinforcement learning, and no review has brought it together around these three decisions. Following PRISMA 2020, the review searched Scopus, IEEE Xplore and the ACM Digital Library. Of 4,399 records it included 34 studies, appraised their reporting quality, and synthesised them around five questions: how the problem is formulated under uncertain evidence, which decisions the agents make, how operational rules are enforced, how agents are evaluated, and how robustness is tested.

The main findings are that explicit belief over a hidden incident state is rare (4 studies), escalation to a person is rarely learned (7 studies include it, and only one combines information gathering, a response action and escalation), and rule enforcement is proposed more often than it is measured (5 studies use a hard filter; 3 report what happens to a blocked decision). The review concludes that a single learned policy that investigates, acts or escalates for IT service incidents under explicit rules has not been reported, and it sets out the evaluation such work needs. I believe this fits the journal's scope of critical, evidence-based reviews of AI methods and their applications.
